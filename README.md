## Project choice
I chose Option A, Café Calle Ocho, because I am interested in building a simple system that makes ordering food and coffee easier. A café ordering application also gives me an opportunity to solve problems for both customers and kitchen employees.

## Problem statement
For this prototype, I assume Café Calle Ocho currently receives many orders at the counter and manages kitchen requests through verbal communication and paper tickets. During busy hours, this can create long lines, confusion about orders, and uncertainty about pickup times for customers and kitchen staff. Our prototype will provide a mobile-friendly digital menu, pickup ordering, order status tracking, and a kitchen board so customers can order more conveniently and employees can prepare orders in an organized way.

## Target users

### Persona 1: Marta Rodriguez - Customer
- **Role:** Regular customer who buys coffee and breakfast before work.
- **Goals:** Order quickly, avoid long lines, and know when her pickup order is ready.
- **Current frustrations:** Waiting in line, unclear pickup times, and repeating her order at the counter.
- **Technology comfort:** Moderate to high; regularly uses her smartphone and mobile websites.
- **Quote:** "I want to order my café con leche before I arrive and pick it up without waiting in a long line."

### Persona 2: Luis Hernandez - Kitchen staff
- **Role:** Kitchen employee responsible for preparing café orders.
- **Goals:** See incoming orders clearly, prepare them in the correct sequence, and mark them ready.
- **Current frustrations:** Hard-to-read paper tickets, verbal order changes, and uncertainty about which orders are completed.
- **Technology comfort:** Moderate; comfortable using simple touchscreen interfaces after a short introduction.
- **Quote:** "I need one clear board that tells me what to prepare next and which orders are ready."

## Must-have prototype features
1. **Digital menu:** Display food and drinks with names, descriptions, and prices.
2. **Pickup order placement:** Let customers select menu items and quantities and submit orders.
3. **Special instructions:** Allow customers to add simple preparation notes.
4. **Order confirmation:** Show a unique order number and an order summary.
5. **Order status tracking:** Show received, preparing, ready, and picked up statuses.
6. **Kitchen board:** Let staff view orders and move them through the status workflow.
7. **Order history and menu administration:** Show past orders and let authorized staff maintain menu items.

## Not in this prototype
1. Online card payments and payment processing.
2. SMS or push notifications.
3. Native iOS or Android mobile applications.
4. Loyalty points and rewards.
5. Delivery-driver dispatch and live delivery tracking.

## Required questions

### Why is a "not in this prototype" list useful?
It helps me stay focused on the main ordering problem instead of trying to build every possible feature at once. It also helps the client understand what the first version will and will not include, so we can agree on realistic expectations.

### Which persona matters most to the business owner, and why?
Both personas matter, but Marta, the customer, is the most important for the business goal because customers create the orders and revenue. If the system makes ordering faster and easier for Marta, she may return more often. Luis is also essential because the kitchen must fulfill orders correctly for customers to have a good experience.
